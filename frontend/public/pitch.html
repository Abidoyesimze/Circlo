Problem

Trust is the whole product — and the whole risk.

Hundreds of millions of people already save this way: a fixed group, a fixed contribution, a fixed rotation — cash handed to an organizer who keeps the notebook and pays out each round.

It works because everyone trusts the organizer. It breaks the same way every time: a missed payment nobody can prove, a dispute over whose turn it is, or the organizer disappearing with the pool entirely.

01

No record of truth

Contributions tracked in a notebook or a WhatsApp chat — memory and goodwill, not proof.

02

One point of failure

Every contribution passes through a single person's hands before it reaches anyone.

03

No recourse

If the organizer mismanages or disappears, the group's savings go with them.

Solution

Same circle. No one holding the money.

Circlo keeps the exact social structure your group already trusts — fixed members, fixed contribution, fixed rotation — and replaces the organizer's role with a Soroban contract that enforces it instead of promising it.

→

No custodian, ever

There is no withdraw function. Funds only ever leave to the address the rotation itself determines.

→

Permissionless settlement

Anyone — a member, a script, a stranger — can settle a ready cycle. No admin decides when or who gets paid.

→

A penalty with teeth

A refundable deposit backs every member. Miss a cycle and arrears are charged against it — a real cost, not a warning.

How it works

Four steps. The contract runs the rotation.

4/4cycle rotation
01 — form

Create or join

Set the terms while the circle is still forming; anyone can join before it starts.

02 — pay in

Contribute

Every member pays into the contract's own balance each cycle — not to a person.

03 — settle

Trigger payout

Once the cycle is complete (or its deadline passes), anyone can settle it.

04 — rotate

Advance

The contract pays the scheduled recipient and rotates to the next cycle itself.

Why Stellar

The fees have to disappear for this to work.

Circles run on small, frequent contributions — weekly or monthly. On most chains, gas alone would eat a meaningful slice of a $10 contribution. Stellar is the one place that isn't true.

→

Sub-cent fees, ~5s finality

Micro-contributions stay practical instead of getting taxed away by the network itself.

→

Native stablecoin rails

USDC on Stellar means contributors deal in stable value, not a volatile asset — essential for a savings product.

→

Soroban enforcement

Smart contracts let Circlo enforce rotation and payout logic on-chain — solving the actual trust problem, not just digitizing it.

Architecture

One contract. Many circles. No shortcuts.

A single deployed instance manages every circle via composite storage keys — no per-group contract, no growing cost per circle. Every write touches only the small piece of state that actually changed.

·

trigger_payout is permissionless

No admin, no off-chain arbiter decides when a cycle settles.

·

Deposit + arrears, not just a revert

A missed contribution charges real collateral instead of blocking the circle.
